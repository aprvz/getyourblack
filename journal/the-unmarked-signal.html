The unmarked signal

In Hong Kong, the loudest cap is the one with nothing on it.

A logo on a cap used to be a shortcut. It told the room what you could afford, and it did it from across the street. That shortcut is tired. On the Star Ferry, in a lift in Central, at a lunch where everyone can buy the same mark, a branded crown no longer says very much. It says you wanted to be seen wanting.

Get Your Black is built for the other instinct. The cap is black. The buckle is plain titanium. There is no embroidery, no patch, no metal word on the strap. If someone across the table notices it, they notice the cloth, the weight of the brim, the fact that the shape has not collapsed in the humidity. They do not notice a name, because there isn’t one.

A costume needs an audience. An unmarked cap assumes you already know who you are.

The name has not been thrown away. It has been moved. It sits on the lid of the box, in a small gold line, with the Chinese of the house beneath it: 得其黑. You see it when the cap arrives. You see it when you put the cap away. You do not see it when you walk out.

Hong Kong is a good place for this kind of object. The light is hard. The air is wet. People stand close. A white logo on a black crown photographs itself onto every window you pass. Take the mark off, and the cap becomes what it should have been: shade, rain, a clean line above the shoulder of a black coat.

There is a particular wealth in this city that has already finished proving itself. It does not need a second logo on the way from the car to the door. It still wants the object to be serious. Serious, here, means the cotton is tight, the brim does not shine, the buckle will still be a buckle in five years, and nobody is paid to recognise it.

The edition is two hundred and fifty. The price sits where a serious cap should sit for this city, at HK$880, inside a range of five hundred to one thousand. It is not a bargain and it is not a boast. It is the cost of six layers, a plain buckle, and a box that does the talking.

We are not selling it on this site. The pages are a viewing room. The cap, when you hold it, should feel like the photographs: matte, structured, quiet. Until then, the signal is the absence.
